The wall by the orchard came down in the March storms. Not all of it, just the forty feet where the old pear leans over it, which is the forty feet everyone sees from the lane.

## What a dry stone wall wants

It wants **two faces and a heart**. The faces are the stones you see. The heart is the rubble you pack between them, and it does most of the work.

- Big stones at the bottom, always.
- Every stone touches the one below in two places.
- *One over two, two over one.* Never stack joints.

I learned most of this from a book my neighbor lent me[^*Stone by Stone*, a small green paperback with a cracked spine.] and the rest from taking things apart.

> A wall is a slow conversation with gravity. You lose every argument eventually, but you can lose them very slowly.

By the third Saturday the wall stood ==knee high==. The pear has opinions about it.
